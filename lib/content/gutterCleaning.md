## How to Estimate Gutter Length

Measure each roof edge with gutters and add the lengths together. Do not measure every roof ridge: only count the eaves where a gutter is installed. A typical single-story rectangular house may have 120 to 200 linear feet of gutters. Two-story homes, detached garages, porches, and additions can add substantially more.

## What Raises the Cost?

Heavy leaf buildup, wet packed debris, pine needles, roof moss, steep roofs, three or more stories, limited ladder placement, landscaping, and poor access all increase labor and safety time. Gutter guards still need maintenance: fine debris can sit on top of the guard or clog the opening beneath it. A downspout flush is useful when water overflows even after the gutter channel has been cleared.

## When to Clean Gutters

Many homes need cleaning twice each year—after spring pollen and after autumn leaf drop. Homes below mature trees may need more frequent service. Schedule cleaning before the rainy season, and inspect the system after major storms. Water spilling behind a gutter, staining siding, or pooling near the foundation may point to a blockage, loose hanger, incorrect slope, or damaged downspout.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does gutter cleaning cost?

For a typical one- or two-story home, professional gutter cleaning often costs $125 to $350. High homes, long gutter runs, difficult access, and heavily compacted debris can increase the quote.

### Are gutter guards maintenance-free?

No. Guards reduce the volume of debris entering the channel, but they can collect leaves and shingle grit. They should be inspected and cleaned as needed.

### Is gutter cleaning worth hiring out?

Professional cleaning can be worthwhile when the roof is steep, the home is taller than one story, access is difficult, or you are not comfortable working on ladders. Ask whether the service includes bagging debris, testing downspouts, and reporting visible gutter damage.
