## Stair Calculator — Complete Stair Framing & Building Code Guide

## How to Calculate Stairs: Step-by-Step (IRC & IBC Code Compliant)

Stair design and framing is governed by precise geometric rules and mandatory building code requirements in every jurisdiction in the United States, Canada, and the UK. Poorly designed stairs cause more residential injuries than any other structural element in the home.

Our free stair calculator computes exact riser height, tread depth, stringer length, incline angle, headroom clearance, handrail linear footage, and baluster counts — fully compliant with **IRC §R311.7** (residential) and **IBC §1011** (commercial).

---

## Key Stair Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Total Rise** | Total vertical height from the lower finished floor to the upper finished floor. This is the only input measurement required. |
| **Riser** | The vertical face of each step (the "height" of each step). |
| **Tread** | The horizontal surface of each step you walk on (the "depth" of each step). |
| **Nosing** | The overhanging edge of the tread projecting beyond the riser face (typically ¾" to 1¼"). |
| **Stringer** | The diagonal structural member (usually 2×12 lumber) cut in a sawtooth pattern to support the treads and risers. |
| **Throat Depth** | The minimum perpendicular distance remaining on the stringer after the tread and riser notches are cut. Must be ≥ 3.5 in (IRC §R311.7.10). |
| **Pitch / Incline Angle** | The angle of the stringer diagonal from horizontal. Ideal comfort range is 30° to 37°. |
| **Headroom** | The vertical clearance measured from the nosing of any tread to the nearest obstruction directly above (ceiling, soffit, or joist). IRC requires minimum 80 in (6 ft 8 in). |
| **Run** | The horizontal distance covered by the entire stair flight (Tread Depth × Number of Treads). |
| **Blondel's Rule** | The classic ergonomic comfort formula: 2 × Riser Height + Tread Depth = 24 to 25 in (600 to 635 mm). |

---

## Step 1 — Determine Total Rise (Most Critical Measurement)

Total Rise is the only site measurement required before any calculation:

> **Total Rise = Upper Finished Floor Level − Lower Finished Floor Level**

Measure from the **finished floor surface** to the **finished floor surface** — not subfloor to subfloor. This accounts for flooring materials (hardwood, tile, carpet) on both levels and prevents accumulated riser height error.

* **Example:** A standard 8 ft ceiling home with 2×10 floor joists (9.25 in actual) and 0.75 in subfloor = 105 in total rise.

---

## Step 2 — Calculate Number of Risers

The number of risers is found by dividing Total Rise by your target riser height and rounding to the nearest whole number:

> **Number of Risers = Round(Total Rise / Target Riser Height)**

* **Example:** 105 in / 7.5 in = 14 risers (rounded)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> All risers in a single flight must be identical height (within 3/8 in tolerance per IRC §R311.7.5.1). Even a small inconsistency causes trips and falls.

---

## Step 3 — Calculate Exact Riser Height

Once the riser count is fixed, the exact riser height is the total rise divided evenly:

> **Exact Riser Height = Total Rise / Number of Risers**

* **Example:** 105 in / 14 risers = **7.5 in per riser** (perfectly uniform)

### Building Code Limits:

| Standard | Maximum Riser Height | Minimum Tread Depth | Typical Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IRC §R311.7** (Residential) | 7.75 in (197 mm) | 10.0 in (254 mm) | Single-family homes, townhomes, apartments |
| **IBC §1011.5** (Commercial) | 7.0 in (178 mm) | 11.0 in (279 mm) | Offices, retail, hotels, schools, assembly |
| **OSHA 1910.25** (Industrial) | 9.5 in (241 mm) | 9.5 in (241 mm) | Industrial stairs and ladderways |

---

## Step 4 — Number of Treads & Total Run

Stairs have one fewer tread than riser in standard residential framing (the upper floor landing acts as the final "tread"):

> **Number of Treads = Number of Risers − 1** (standard; down-one-riser mounting)

> **Total Horizontal Run = Number of Treads × Tread Depth**

* **Example:** 13 treads × 10.5 in = **136.5 in (11.375 ft) total run**

---

## Step 5 — Stringer Layout (2×12 Diagonal Cut Length)

The stringer is the critical load-bearing component. Its diagonal length is calculated using the Pythagorean theorem:

> **Stringer Length = √(Total Rise² + Total Run²)**

* **Example:** √(105² + 136.5²) = √(11025 + 18632.25) = √29657.25 = **172.2 in (14.35 ft)**
* Recommended lumber: **2×12 × 16 ft** (next standard length above 14.35 ft)

### Stringer Throat Depth (Minimum 3.5 in)

After cutting the tread and riser notches, the remaining perpendicular depth of the stringer (the "throat") must not be less than **3.5 inches** per IRC §R311.7.10. This is the most common cause of stringer failure on steep stairs.

---

## Step 6 — Stair Pitch Angle & Ergonomic Comfort

### Pitch Angle

> **Stair Angle = arctan(Riser Height / Tread Depth)**

* **Example:** arctan(7.5 / 10.5) = **35.5°** (ideal comfort zone)

| Angle Range | Ergonomic Rating | Application |
|---|---|---|
| < 25° | Very gradual (ramp-like) | Accessibility ramps, grand staircases |
| 30° – 37° | **Ideal comfortable range** | Standard residential & commercial stairs |
| 37° – 42° | Moderate (slightly steep) | Basement stairs, deck stairs, loft ladders |
| > 42° | Steep (attic-style) | Storage loft stairs, alternating tread stairs |

### Blondel's Ergonomic Rule (1675 AD)

The Blondel Rule balances the step height and depth for natural human gait. For every stride, the leg rises and also moves forward — the ideal balance is:

> **2R + T = 24 to 25 in (600 to 635 mm metric)**

Where R = Riser Height and T = Tread Depth.

* **Example:** 2 × 7.5 + 10.5 = **25.5 in** (slightly above ideal — widen tread by 0.25 in for perfect comfort)

---

## Step 7 — Stairwell Opening Length (Headroom Clearance)

IRC §R311.7.2 mandates **80 in (6 ft 8 in) minimum vertical headroom** measured from the nosing of each tread to the ceiling or floor joist above. To ensure compliance, the stairwell ceiling opening must be long enough.

> **Minimum Opening Length = Total Run − ((Total Rise − Min Headroom − Floor Joist Thickness) / tan(Pitch Angle))**

This formula calculates how far back from the upper floor edge the stairwell hole must begin to prevent any tread nosing from being within 80 in of the ceiling above.

---

## Step 8 — Handrails & Balusters

### Handrail Requirements (IRC §R311.7.8)
- Required on all stairs with **4 or more risers**.
- Grip diameter: **1.25 in – 2 in** (circular) or within 4 in perimeter for non-circular profiles.
- Height: **34 in – 38 in** above tread nosing.
- **Handrail Length ≈ Stringer Length + 12 in** (for top and bottom horizontal returns).

### Baluster / Spindle Requirements (IRC §R312.1.3)
- Maximum **4 in sphere** must not pass between balusters (prevents child head entrapment).
- **Balusters per Tread = Ceil(Tread Depth / 4 in)**
- **Total Balusters = Balusters per Tread × Number of Treads**

## Worked Example — Full Stair Calculation

**Project:** Basement stair, 105 in total rise, 36 in wide, IRC residential code, vinyl plank flooring on both levels.

| Parameter | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of Risers** | Round(105 / 7.5) | **14 risers** |
| **Exact Riser Height** | 105 / 14 | **7.5 in** |
| **Number of Treads** | 14 − 1 | **13 treads** |
| **Total Run** | 13 × 10.5 | **136.5 in (11.4 ft)** |
| **Stringer Length** | √(105² + 136.5²) | **172.2 in (14.35 ft)** |
| **Recommended Lumber** | Next 2 ft above 14.35 ft | **2×12 × 16 ft** |
| **Number of Stringers** | Ceil(36" / 16" o.c.) + 1 | **3 stringers** |
| **Pitch Angle** | arctan(7.5 / 10.5) | **35.5°** |
| **Blondel Check** | 2(7.5) + 10.5 | **25.5 in** (pass, ≤ 25.5 acceptable) |
| **Headroom Opening** | Calculated at 80" clearance + 10.5" joist | **≈ 98 in (8.2 ft)** |
| **Handrail Length** | 172.2 + 12 | **≈ 15.4 ft** |
| **Baluster Count** | Ceil(10.5 / 4) × 13 = 3 × 13 | **39 balusters** |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many steps do I need for 8 ft ceilings?
A standard 8 ft (96 in) ceiling with typical floor structure results in approximately **105 in total rise** (including floor joists, subfloor, and upper flooring). This typically produces **14 risers** at 7.5 in each.

### What is the ideal riser height?
The ideal riser height for comfortable residential stairs is **7 in to 7.5 in**. IRC maximum is 7.75 in; going above this creates a steeper, more fatiguing stair. IBC commercial maximum is 7.0 in.

### How do I calculate stair stringers for deck stairs?
Deck stairs often have a larger total rise depending on deck height. Use the same formula: divide total rise by your target riser height, round to the nearest whole number for riser count, then multiply tread count by 10 in to 11 in for total run. Cut 2×12 stringers using a framing square set to your exact riser and tread dimensions.

### What size lumber do I need for stringers?
Standard cut stringers use **2×12 dimensional lumber** (actual width 11.25 in). The remaining throat depth after cutting must be at least 3.5 in. For very long or steep stairs, consult a structural engineer for LVL (Laminated Veneer Lumber) stringers.

### How do I calculate headroom for basement stairs?
Measure from the floor joist bottom (not the drywall ceiling) to the step nosing along the stair pitch line. If the lowest nosing-to-joist measurement is under 80 in (IRC), you need to either relocate the stair opening or reduce the number of risers to reduce the stair angle.
